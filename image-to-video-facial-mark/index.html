One-detail review

Does one facial mark stay in place in image to video?

The short answer

Mark the small feature in the source image, request one restrained movement, then inspect the first, middle, and final frames for its position and count. It should remain on the same visible cheek beside the same facial landmark, and appear only once. A moved or duplicated mark fails this brief. If that cheek is hidden, the frame is inconclusive.

This is an acceptance check, not a promise that a prompt can control every generator.

Write a source anchor that survives a small motion

“Keep the face the same” is too broad to tell you whether one tiny detail survived. On a copy of the source, circle only the feature you care about. Record which cheek it belongs to in the source image, the nearby eye or mouth corner, and whether there is exactly one such mark. A plain screen-left or screen-right label becomes unreliable during a head turn, so the first test should keep the head near its source pose.

The diagrams here use an invented face. They show how to inspect a result; they do not claim to be outputs from an image-to-video model or tests on a real person's photo.

Give the model one motion job

For the first pass, keep the camera steady and try a blink or a slight change in gaze. A large head turn makes the cheek harder to compare and may hide it completely. Runway's Gen-4 prompting guide says the input image establishes the visual starting point and recommends simple, direct motion wording. That guidance is specific to its workflow; it does not guarantee the mark will hold in this or another model.

Example test promptThe person holds the source pose and blinks once. The one small mark visible on the cheek in the reference image stays beside the same eye throughout the shot. The camera remains stationary.

The prompt states the intended result. The frame check below decides whether the actual clip met it. Adding more synonyms for “same” cannot turn a failed output into a pass.

Judge three comparable views

  1. Start: match the visible mark to the circled source position and count it.
  2. Middle: pause when the cheek is visible, then check whether the mark stays beside the same eye or mouth corner.
  3. End: count again. One mark at the original relative position is the target; a copy or a jump is not.
  4. Occlusion: if hair, a hand, or a turn hides the cheek, mark that frame inconclusive and inspect another visible moment. Do not call an unseen mark stable.

Questions people ask

Why did the mark switch sides?First check whether the whole image is mirrored or the head turned. Compare frames at a similar pose. If the mark has changed cheeks relative to the source, the output does not meet this brief; the frames alone do not reveal the model's cause.
What if it disappears for a moment?A hidden cheek is inconclusive. If the cheek is visible and the mark is absent, reject that interval for this brief.
Will a stronger prompt fix duplication?There is no guaranteed phrase. Retry with a clearer source and less motion, then count the mark in the new result.
