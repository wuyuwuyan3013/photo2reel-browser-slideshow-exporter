Practical image to video guide

How to keep the background consistent in image to video.

Define the current setting as fixed, lock the camera, and animate just one foreground detail instead of asking the scene to evolve.

The practical background rule

Name the existing background, lighting, and framing as unchanged, then request one small movement that does not require a new place or point of view. Remove words such as travel, reveal, transition, arrival, cut, or time-lapse when the source setting must remain stable.

Use the source image as the setting reference

Background drift often begins when a prompt describes a story instead of a short motion. A clear source frame already contains the location, objects, light, and composition. Preserve those details first, then add a small movement that can happen within the existing scene.

Stable requestKeep the room, furniture, window light, and framing unchanged while curtains move gently.
Unstable requestTake the viewer through the room, reveal the city outside, then change to evening.
Low-risk movementSteam, leaves, rain, fabric, a reflection, or distant light movement.
High-risk movementWalking into another space, a camera orbit, a new subject entering, or a change of weather.

Write a fixed-setting prompt

State the background limits before the action. Be specific about what already exists in the image, but keep the movement instruction short. One direct exclusion list helps prevent an unintended new location from becoming part of the clip.

Background preservation promptKeep the exact room, walls, furniture, window, lighting, and camera framing from the reference image. Locked camera. The person remains in place and only the curtain moves slowly in a light breeze. No new scenery, no location change, no time-of-day change, no camera pan, no zoom, no cuts, and no new objects or people.

Build the result in four checks

  1. Choose a readable scene. Use a source image where the setting is visible enough to recognize after motion begins.
  2. Lock the viewpoint. Keep the camera and crop fixed before testing any foreground action.
  3. Add one local movement. Make the action occur inside the current frame, such as a curtain, cloud, reflection, or breath.
  4. Remove story words first. If the setting changes, cut words that imply travel, discovery, transformation, or a new time.

Fix common background changes

A new location appearsUse a stronger instruction to keep the existing room or landscape unchanged and remove transition language.
The lighting turns into another timeAsk for stable lighting and avoid sunrise, sunset, storm, neon, or cinematic time-change descriptions.
The view drifts awayUse “locked camera” and remove pan, orbit, tracking, zoom, handheld, or reveal.
Extra objects appearRequest no new objects or people, then simplify the action to a single visible movement.

Let atmosphere move without moving the setting

A stable background does not have to look frozen. Small environmental movement can make a scene feel alive while protecting its location and composition. Test that restrained version before adding a second action or a more stylized treatment.
