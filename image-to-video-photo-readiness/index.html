PRECHECK
Before the motion prompt

Is one photo ready for image-to-video?

A tool may accept one image, but acceptance is not the same as a motion-ready source. Check what a viewer can identify, what the crop protects, and what the scene gives the model room to move.

The short answer

A photo is motion-ready when its subject, crop, and depth cues can survive a small change over time. If the subject is unclear, the essential detail touches the edge, or every object sits on one flat plane, repair the source before you write the prompt.

Run three checks before generation

This is not a quality score. It is a short way to decide whether a still has enough usable information for the specific movement you want. A ready photo can still produce an imperfect clip; the check simply prevents an avoidable source problem from being misread as a prompt problem.

Check one

Subject

Name the one thing that must remain recognisable after movement starts. If two people, two products, or a crowded background compete for attention, decide which one owns the first moment.

Check two

Crop

Look for protected space around the detail that matters. A face, label, hand, or product edge pressed against the frame cannot safely absorb a camera move or an aspect-ratio crop.

Check three

Depth

Find at least two visible layers that could move differently: foreground and background, subject and setting, or a stable surface and a moving element. This gives motion a relationship to describe.

Fix the source, not the motion sentence

If the subject is unclear

  • Choose a version with one obvious visual owner.
  • Remove a competing crop or choose a different first frame.
  • Write the motion around the chosen subject, not the whole scene.

If the crop is too tight

  • Use a wider source instead of asking the model to invent missing edges.
  • Reserve room around a product label, face, or action point.
  • Choose the intended aspect ratio before checking the edge space.

If the scene is flat

  • Look for an image with a foreground, middle distance, and background.
  • Use environmental motion only when a visible layer supports it.
  • Keep the requested camera move modest when depth is weak.

If a detail must not drift

  • Name the detail in the source record before prompting.
  • Make it large enough to inspect in the intended delivery frame.
  • Review that one detail before judging the rest of the result.

A four-step readiness preflight

  1. Choose the intended frame. Decide whether the finished clip is vertical, square, or landscape before you judge the crop. The same photo can be ready for one route and too tight for another.
  2. Point to the visual owner. State what must still be recognised after the first movement. If you cannot identify it in one phrase, simplify the source choice.
  3. Mark the protected edge. Identify the detail that cannot be lost to a crop, tilt, or camera move. Replace the source or widen the composition if there is no margin.
  4. Name one relationship that can move. Describe an observable pair, such as a stationary product against a changing light or a person against a distant background. Then test that one idea first.

Why one image is not automatically enough

An archived TokkingHeads page from 2021 described a one-photo animation product that accepted a single image alongside other animation inputs. That is a capability boundary, not a promise that every uploaded photo is ready for every kind of motion. This guide turns that boundary into a source-photo check: first establish what the image can preserve, then choose a movement that fits it.

Questions about image-to-video photo readiness

Can a portrait be motion-ready if the background is plain?

Yes, if the face is clearly framed and the motion is modest. Treat the person and the background as separate visual layers, and do not ask a flat image to support a large camera move it cannot show.

Should I add empty space around the subject?

Add it only where the intended crop or movement needs it. The goal is not a generic border; it is enough protected room for the details you must keep visible in the chosen frame.

What if I want several different beats?

Use a storyboard or separate source images when each beat needs a different composition. A single photo is a useful starting scene, not a substitute for source decisions you have not made.

Does this check guarantee a stable result?

No. It gives you a clearer source and a narrower first test. Keep a record of the source, route, and chosen review question so the next version has a visible basis for comparison.
