Contact check

How do you keep one hand-object contact believable in image to video?

The short answer

Describe one contact relationship before you generate: where the hand begins, what one action may change, and what the last visible frame must still prove. Then compare the start, middle, and end for the same object, the same contact side, and a hand that has not gained, lost, or detached a finger.

This is a review method for a small motion test, not a promise that a model will preserve a grip.

Write a contact contract, not a crowded action scene

Hand contact is harder to judge when the brief asks for a lift, twist, camera push, new angle, and a changing background in the same short clip. Start by naming the relationship that is already visible: which hand touches which part of which object, from which side, and where the object sits in the frame. Then allow one action that changes that relationship.

Runway’s current UGC video guide specifically tells creators to inspect hands closely when a hand turns or opens a product. That is workflow-specific guidance, not a universal model test. The reusable idea here is to turn a hard-to-describe contact into three visible checks.

Contact contractSource fact: The right hand holds the bottle around its lower half; the thumb is visible on the near side, and the bottle base rests on the table.
Allowed event: The hand lifts the bottle straight up once. The camera, bottle label, and contact side remain stable until the bottle leaves the frame.

Review three frames, not just the most attractive moment

Pause on the first clear frame, a middle frame where movement is obvious, and the last frame before the object exits or settles. Each frame answers a different question. If the middle contact cannot be read because the hand covers the object, call it inconclusive instead of calling it a pass.

Keep one motion owner

Hand lifts objectThe hand owns the change. Hold the camera and object identity steady; do not also ask for a turntable rotation.
Hand presses objectThe contact point owns the change. Keep the object’s screen, label, or surface in one readable orientation.
Hand opens objectThe opening owns the change. Do not bundle it with a new viewpoint or an unpictured interior.
Object moves without a handUse this only when the source does not need a visible hand relationship. It is a different test with different proof.

A small retry ladder

  1. Remove the camera move. A fixed viewpoint makes the contact side and object position easier to compare.
  2. Reduce the event. Ask for a lift, press, or open—not a lift plus turn plus reveal.
  3. Protect one visible reference. Keep a label, rim, button, edge, or object base in view through the event.
  4. Name the usable last frame. Decide whether the object is lifted, settled, or still on the surface before the test begins.

Questions people ask

Can I ask a hand to pick up an object and show its back?That joins a contact event with an unpictured angle. Prepare another source frame if the back is essential to the viewer.
What if the hand covers the important label?Do not grade that middle frame as proof. Move the protected reference, reduce the action, or choose a less occluding source composition.
Why check three frames?One clean still can hide a broken transition. Start, middle, and end show whether the same contact survives the requested change.
